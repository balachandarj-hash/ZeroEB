Whose roof it is

A terrace with one owner's name.

A community of several hundred flats cannot lend its terrace to one bill. The tanks, the lifts, and the common services already use that slab. Adding a private row for a single flat would sit on property the others also own. If the association later asks for a plant that serves the block, that is a separate project. It does not start from one resident's enquiry.

Inland from the highway, and in villa layouts, the slab usually has one name. That is the survey. The tank stays. A walkway stays. The row follows the daytime bill. City-wide module choice is on panels for a Chennai home. A mast on a smaller block is the Perungudi case. Two families in one house is the Kovilambakkam case. Here the question is whether the roof is yours or the community's.

Who it suits

A house or a villa, not a balcony in a tower.

Independent houses off the highway are the usual fit. A villa in a small layout is a fit when the terrace is in your name, not held in common with every neighbour. A flat in a large community waits, unless the association itself wants a shared plant. A balcony facing the road is not a mounting place.

How the row is fixed on a roof you own is on solar panel installation. Modules you already hold are labour, quoted on installation services. The subsidy gap is on panel and price. NDCR against DCR is in that guide before an import is confirmed.

Shape before wattage

Fit the rectangle. Leave the corners.

Older houses around Keelkattalai eri, and some of the newer ones on the same irregular plots, do not give a brochure roof. One side steps in for a neighbour's wall. Another corner is cut by the stair room. Chasing that crooked edge with extra modules wastes glass and blocks the tank. We measure the largest clear rectangle, put the row there, and stop.

The lake changes the calendar more than the drawing. Some lanes hold water after a heavy rain. A design can be finished then. A lorry cannot. We book delivery for a day the street is passable. City-wide module choice is on panels for a Chennai home. Whether the slab is the last floor is the Medavakkam question. Wind off open wetland is the Pallikaranai case. Here the limits are the shape of the plot and the day a vehicle can arrive.

Before the lorry is booked

Shape, the lane, then the subsidy.

Do you visit Keelkattalai from Madipakkam?

Yes. The roof visit is free. A recent bill and a terrace photo are enough to start. No modules are ordered that day.

The parapet is not a rectangle. Can modules still fit?

Yes, inside the largest clear rectangle. The odd corners stay open for the tank and a walkway. We do not cut modules to follow the crooked edge.

Our lane floods when the eri rises. Does that stop the plant?

It stops the delivery day, not the design. We book the install when a vehicle can reach the house.

Do subsidy plants need DCR modules?

Yes. Cells and modules must be made in India. Eligible homes can receive up to ₹78,000, subject to the scheme on the day you apply. NDCR is only if that assistance has already been given up.

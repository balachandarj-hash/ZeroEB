Do You Lose Your EB Connection After Solar? No — Here's Why

How solar works · 26 August 2026 · 5 min read

This is one of the most common worries we hear on a first visit: if I install solar, do I have to surrender my electricity board connection, and what happens on a cloudy day or at night? The short answer is that a standard residential rooftop system is designed to work with the grid. You keep your connection, and the two simply share the load.

How a grid-tied system actually behaves

Through the day, your panels generate DC power, the inverter converts it to AC, and your home consumes that first. Nothing in the house behaves differently — the fan, the fridge, and the air conditioner run exactly as before.

The switching between these states is automatic and instantaneous. You will not notice it happening.

Where the net meter comes in

A grid-connected residential installation is normally paired with a net meter, which records both the electricity you draw from the grid and the surplus you export to it. Your billing is then based on the net position rather than on your gross consumption. This is exactly why the EB connection stays: without it there is nowhere for the surplus to go and nothing to fall back on after sunset.

Worth confirming for your own case: metering arrangements, applicable tariffs, and the treatment of exported units are set by the distribution utility and can change. We will walk you through the current position for your connection during the site visit.

Why going fully off-grid rarely makes sense at home

Cutting the connection entirely means storing every unit you need for the evening and for stretches of bad weather, which requires a substantial battery bank. Batteries add significant cost, occupy space, and need replacement well before the panels do. For a home in a city with a reliable supply, the economics almost never justify it. Off-grid genuinely suits remote sites without a usable connection — not a house in Madipakkam.

If your concern is power cuts rather than billing, the right conversation is about a hybrid system with a modest battery for essential circuits, which is a different design from a plain grid-tied plant.

What changes on your bill

Your consumption from the grid falls, most noticeably for daytime usage. Fixed charges tied to your service connection continue to apply, so a well-sized system reduces your bill substantially rather than erasing every line on it. Sizing matters here: a system built around your actual annual consumption gives a better return than one sized to chase a zero figure, which is why we always ask for a year of bills before recommending a capacity.
